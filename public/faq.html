Passport FAQs

Quick answers about passport applications, renewals, lost or damaged passports, appointments, and pickup.

For guidance only. This site is not the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA). Requirements and procedures can change and may depend on your case. Confirm the latest checklist, appointment rules, and fees through the official DFA Consular Affairs website or the official passport appointment portal.

First-Time Applications

Start with a confirmed appointment, your completed application form, your PSA-issued birth certificate, and a valid government-issued ID. Bring the required originals and photocopies. Check the current DFA checklist for your applicant type before your appointment, since additional documents may apply.

The DFA's accepted-ID list can change. Examples commonly listed include a PhilID/ePhilID, driver's license, SSS or GSIS ID, UMID, PRC ID, and Postal ID. Bring a valid original ID and a photocopy, and verify that your specific ID is currently accepted on the official DFA website before you go.

Passport Renewal

Generally, prepare a confirmed appointment, the completed application form, and your current or most recently issued ePassport with a photocopy of its data page. Bring any additional documents listed for your situation in the DFA renewal checklist.

Renew well ahead of your trip so there is time for an appointment and processing. Many destinations and airlines require at least six months of passport validity, but rules vary. Check the entry requirements of every destination and transit country, and do not rely on an estimated processing period as a guarantee.

Lost or Damaged Passports

Report the loss to the appropriate authorities and follow the DFA's lost-passport process. A police report and a notarized Affidavit of Loss may be required. Book the appropriate appointment and check the DFA checklist for your case; the required documents can depend on whether the passport was still valid and the circumstances of the loss.

Do not assume a damaged passport can be handled as a routine renewal. Contact or consult the DFA for the applicable damaged-passport requirements before booking. Keep the damaged passport and prepare any supporting documents specified for your case.

Appointments & Pickups

No. An appointment reserves a time to submit your application; the DFA still needs to review your documents and application. Bring the required documents and follow the instructions in your official appointment confirmation.

Authorized pickup may be allowed in some cases, subject to DFA rules. The representative may need an authorization letter or Special Power of Attorney, the applicant's original receipt, and valid IDs for both parties. Requirements can differ for minors and other circumstances, so confirm the current pickup rules with the releasing office.

Follow the appearance and photo instructions in your appointment confirmation and those provided by the DFA staff. Keep your face clearly visible and be ready to remove items that obstruct the photo or biometric capture. Check the latest official guidance for specific clothing, eyewear, and accessory rules.
